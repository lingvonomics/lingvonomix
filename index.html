Lesson 1: Introduction to Economics

1. What is economics?
Economics is a social science that studies the production, distribution and consumption of goods and services.

Today, modern economists tend to divide themselves into two schools: micro- and macroeconomics.

2. Micro- and macroeconomics
Microeconomics is a branch of economics that studies individual consumers, households, specific companies, and markets. Its key questions are: How is the price of a product formed? How does a firm maximize its profit?

Macroeconomics, on the other hand, studies the overall economy as a whole. Its key questions are: What is the national inflation rate? How is the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) changing?

3. Bonus: Historical background - The invisible hand
The invisible hand is a metaphor for the principle that people act out of personal gain, yet their pursuit leads them to benefit more for society than they've originally intended. Because every producer pursues their own interests, the beneficial social order arises as an unintended consequence of individuals' actions, since the path to fulfilling their interests actually lies through satisfying someone else's needs. It is important to notice that the metaphor now serves as a theory of market self-regulation and is not a universal economic law but rather an occasional outcome in which a self-interested action frequently could lead to the greater good.

The metaphor is inspired by a fragment from Adam Smith's 1776 book "An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations". In it, Adam Smith makes a mix of micro- and macroeconomic statements.
